PCOS Ultrasound SimCLR + BYOL Duplicate-Aware Splits Reliability Audit

Abstract

Public PCOS ultrasound datasets can support rapid AI research, but duplicated images, pHash near-duplicates, label conflicts, and preprocessing artifacts can inflate headline performance. We evaluate PCOS-XAI ultrasound classification with a reliability-centered protocol: exact and perceptual duplicate auditing, cross-label group exclusion, duplicate-aware train/validation/test splits, supervised transfer baselines, SimCLR and BYOL self-supervised encoders, validation-selected operating points, confidence intervals, calibration, corruption robustness, and Grad-CAM sanity checks. The main finding is nuanced: supervised ResNet-18 remains the strongest low-label default, while SimCLR and BYOL become competitive at 50% labels when threshold selection is handled carefully.

Key Message

Clean accuracy and AUROC are not enough for duplicate-heavy medical imaging datasets.

Why Thresholds Matter

AUROC was almost saturated for strong checkpoints. Validation-selected thresholds better expose sensitivity, specificity, and screening tradeoffs than a default 0.5 threshold alone.

Calibration + Robustness

Calibration improved some checkpoints, but corruption severity showed that clean accuracy did not guarantee stability under blur, downsampling, contrast changes, crop, or noise.

Interpretation

  • Supervised ResNet-18 is the safest low-label default at 10% labels.
  • SimCLR and BYOL become competitive at 50% labels after validation thresholding.
  • Reliability checks reveal failure modes that clean accuracy hides.
  • Grad-CAM should be reported with sanity tests, not as standalone clinical proof.

Conclusion

In low-cleanliness PCOS ultrasound classification, the evaluation protocol is as important as the model: duplicate-aware splitting, confidence intervals, calibration, robustness, and XAI sanity checks make the result more trustworthy than leaderboard-style accuracy alone.
